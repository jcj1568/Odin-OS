# Humanizer — De-AI Editing Pass

Purpose: run a final editing pass on already-good content to remove patterns that read as machine-generated, without changing the substance or making claims the source material doesn't support.

## Tells to remove
- **Rule-of-three overload**: "fast, reliable, and scalable" — vary sentence rhythm instead of stacking triads everywhere.
- **Hedge-stacking**: "it's important to note that," "in today's fast-paced world," "at the end of the day" — cut, don't soften.
- **Em-dash overuse** as a crutch for every aside — use periods or commas more often.
- **Symmetric paragraph lengths** — real writing is uneven; let some paragraphs run two sentences and others run six.
- **Over-signposted structure**: "Firstly... Secondly... In conclusion..." in short-form copy — only keep explicit signposting in long structured docs (like an audit report) where it aids scanning.
- **Empty intensifiers**: "truly," "incredibly," "significantly" used without a number or specific backing.
- **Generic AI-agency language**: "unlock the power of," "leverage cutting-edge," "revolutionize your business" — replace with concrete, specific claims. This especially applies to Aelix copy, where the brand voice is grounded in the Three Levers, not hype.

## What NOT to do
- Don't add typos, grammatical errors, or "imperfections" to fake human authorship — that's deception, not editing.
- Don't strip specificity, data, or sourced claims in the name of sounding casual.
- Don't change the actual argument or recommendation — this is a style pass, not a content rewrite.
- Don't apply this to the audit report's structural sections (executive summary, effort-vs-impact matrix, etc.) — those benefit from clear signposting; apply the tell-removal only to the prose within them.

## How to use it
When asked to humanize a draft:
1. Keep every factual claim, number, and recommendation intact.
2. Rewrite sentence-by-sentence for rhythm variation and tell removal.
3. Flag anything you cut for being ungrounded hype rather than just an AI tic — that's a content issue, not a style one, and James should know about it separately.
